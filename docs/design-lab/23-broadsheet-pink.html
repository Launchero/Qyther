23 · Broadsheet Pink — the financial newspaper, specified from the FT's own token package

Provenance — every hue is quoted from the npm package @financial-times/o-colors 6.7.1, file src/scss/_palette.scss, the FT's published palette, rather than recalled. Primary black #000000, white #ffffff, ft-pink #fcd0b1, ft-grey #333333; secondary claret #990f3d, teal #0d7680, oxford #0f5499, slate #262a33, paper #fff1e5; tertiary mandarin #ff8833, light-blue #00a0dd, crimson #cc0000, wheat #f2dfce, candy #ff7faa, wasabi #96cc28, jade #00994d, velvet #593380, lemon #ffec1a, sky #cce6ff. The package also carries brand-ft-pink, a deprecated alias — do not treat it as a second colour. State the correction plainly: the stock is #fcd0b1, not the #F7E7E0 that circulates in "FT pink" palettes, and the reading field is paper #fff1e5. That distinction is the whole build: pink appears here only as the 3px masthead band and the footer strip, because a #fcd0b1 background destroys the surface ramp — white on it is 1.11:1 and wheat on it 1.10:1, so card, popover and muted collapse into the page and there is nothing left to elevate. Why the paper is pink at all — Gobraithwaite's note on the FT's own history — is an accident of 1893 stock rather than a decision: the paper was printed on leftover pink stock previously used for the envelopes of a fireworks-fuse manufacturer, and the colour stayed because readers had begun to treat it as the mark of the genuine article. That is the honest reason a pink field cannot carry a UI ramp and only a band can: it was never chosen for contrast. SECONDARY SOURCE — the story is attribution, not a value; no primary FT document was fetched for it this session, and unlike every hex above it is not re-derivable from the package file. Licence: the palette is FT copyright published in a public npm package; every contrast ratio in this block is DERIVED in this session from those hexes by the WCAG relative-luminance formula, not published by the FT.

Type plan — the FT's face is Financier Text by Klim, drawn for the 2014 FT redesign and sold as a retail font: proprietary, not shippable. The honest substitutes are all verified SIL OFL 1.1: Oswald or Anton for the condensed gothic headlines, and Playfair Display, Libre Bodoni or Bodoni Moda for the high-contrast news serif body and rows. Scale as planned: headline/deck/sub-deck 26 / 22 / 17 in Oswald 300–500 uppercase tracked, row titles 14, rows 13, meta and paths 12 mono, drop cap 44px (= 3.2em on 14px prose). No webfont is embedded in this mock: on a machine without Oswald the headline stack falls to Haettenschweiler → Arial Narrow → Impact (all condensed gothics, so the rhythm survives), and without Playfair the body falls to Bodoni MT → Didot → Georgia (all high-contrast serifs). A sandboxed renderer would have to vendor the OFL files; nothing here claims that is free.

Geometry — radius 0 on every surface, shadow off (--elev: none, --elev-sm: none, --key: none): elevation is a warm lightness step plus a rule, never depth. 1px hairline column rules as border-inline-end on grid children — the row is a genuine four-column grid (icon | title | path | kind | key) with display: contents dissolving the title/path wrapper so the newspaper column rules can fall between them. Double rules (3px double) break sections; the masthead foot is bold-over-hairline (2px solid + an inset hairline). Line-height is a fixed 20px everywhere, so the vertical rhythm is typographic rather than spacing-token. The row floor stays 40px and the control floor 32px — WCAG target size, not taste.

  • Move 1 · colour, token override onlyRewrite the :root and .dark blocks in src/renderer/styles/semantic/theme.css to the quoted stock — --background: paper #fff1e5, --card: #ffffff, --secondary: wheat #f2dfce, --muted: #e9d3bd (DERIVED), --primary: claret #990f3d, --ring: teal #0d7680, --input: slate #262a33 — plus two new published pairs, --masthead: ft-pink #fcd0b1 and --hairline. Jade and mandarin cannot be lifted to text and stay fills, so --success/--warning are lightness-only derivatives, exactly the move --file-* already makes. No components/ui/ edit.
  • Move 2 · flat + rule-driventokens/radius.css to zero across the scale (it moves the pristine primitives too), tokens/shadow.css emptied to none for --shadow-command-palette, --shadow-popover, --shadow-dialog, --shadow-tooltip, --shadow-shortcut-key; --border stays a decorative tan at 1.61:1 while --input becomes full slate at 12.98:1 — the same decorative/control split, pushed further.
  • Move 3 · type and rhythmtokens/typography.css gains a condensed display stack and a high-contrast serif stack, and the six-rung scale becomes 12/13/14/17/22/26 with a paired 20px line-height on every rung; tokens/spacing.css keeps --target-row and --target-min and drops nothing else. Markup needs four additions, all listed under the surfaces below: the masthead strip, the column heads, the dateline rule, and one ::first-letter drop cap on the settled answer paragraph.

Cost: MEDIUM-LOW — colours (a warm field with rules doing all the elevation), one display face, radius to 0, shadows off, and the primitives handled by token override with no components/ui/ edits.

Biggest risk: stated honestly — two columns of 13px text do not fit 800 × 74 DIP, so this direction is only truthful in expanded mode, and the compact card needs a designed reduced state (masthead plus the query on one line, no rows at all) rather than a clipped version of this one. The second half of the risk is the second theme: there is no honest dark edition — the FT publishes no dark palette, so the night paper below is authored, not inverted (warm black newsprint, wheat as ink, and the three tertiary hues that genuinely clear 4.5:1 there and fail on paper: mandarin 7.81:1, wasabi 9.70:1, sky 14.45:1). Judge it as the weak half: it is a convincing newspaper at a glance and it is one designer's guess, not a quoted source.
