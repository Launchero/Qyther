19 · High-Performance HMI — the operator-station grey field, where colour is an alarm and nothing else

Provenance — five documents, quoted rather than paraphrased. Rockwell Automation, Process HMI Style Guide: “Display background: use a non-saturated background color such as light gray as it will have minimum interference with other color choices”; “only icons that indicate alarm conditions should use colors in the red and yellow spectrum”; and the rule the signature device below lifts verbatim — “Unacknowledged alarms should blink between alarm color and gray border, while acknowledged alarms should continually display alarm color border”. ISA-101 High Performance HMI overview: “Screens depicting the operation running normally should not be covered in brightly saturated colors.” Adroit’s ISA-101 summary: grey backgrounds minimise glare, process lines are dark grey or black, “emphasis is provided by differences in line thickness, not colour”, colour only for alarm conditions. Inductive Automation, Ignition docs: “Gray-scale colors are used instead of the traditional bright red, green and blue.” IEC 60073 restricts indicators to RED, YELLOW, GREEN, BLUE, BLACK, GREY, WHITE — red = danger/faulty, yellow = abnormal/caution, green = safe/normal, blue = mandatory — with the positional rule that red sits topmost. These are vendor manuals and standards overviews: the text is copyrighted to Rockwell/ISA/Adroit/IA and is quoted here for research; there is no package to install and no font licence to buy.

One honest caveat. ISA-18.2 defines alarm priorities; as far as can be established it does not mandate colours, and the five-level P0–P4 hue map some consultancies publish is a convention, not a standard. So the colour-to-severity table on surface 3 is this direction’s own documented contract — it is our mapping, published as such, borrowing IEC 60073’s permitted set. The background and structure values are marked DERIVED: #C9CDD2 field (deliberately neither white nor black — this is the one direction in the lab whose ground is not a lightness ramp), #6C7278 structures, 2px #2E3338 primary lines, #2F5B7A nominal data, alarms only #C1272D/#E8A317, #9BA1A8 off-state.

Type plan — Inter (OFL 1.1, 48 KB Latin variable, vendor it: the sandboxed renderer has no CDN) and otherwise the system stack exactly as it ships. Rows 13/17, values 12/16 in the mono stack, every numeral behind font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so a live meter does not shimmy; rows in the 32–40 px band with 16 px column padding. This family tolerates 13 px better than any other mock here, and the reason is measurable rather than stylistic: text is dark-on-light at 10.59:1 on the field with the secondary ink at 5.52:1 (dim room: 11.21:1 and 6.24:1), and because colour is reserved for alarms there is no low-contrast tinted label competing with the small type. Every text/surface pair actually painted here was computed from the hex, not estimated, and the floor is 4.50:1 — the tightest pair in the whole set is the dark muted foreground on the popover plate. Caveat against this repo: src/renderer/styles/AGENTS.md forbids a size below 12px and the shipped scale starts at 12/14 — adopting 13/17 as the row rung means editing that law, so the safe adoption profile keeps 14/18 rows and takes only the weight and letter-spacing discipline. The 11px alarm-level tags are fills, not prose.

Geometry — 8 px grid, radius 4 px on everything that is not a card, zero shadows (--elev/--elev-sm/--key are none), and a strict two-value line hierarchy: 2px is “this matters”, 1px is “this exists”. Emphasis therefore comes from stroke weight, never from hue. There is no lightness ramp doing the work: --card sits above --background and --popover above that, but a raised panel is announced by a 2px structure border, not by depth — --border is a decorative 1.63:1 divider (dark 1.68:1) and --input a 3.98:1 control boundary (dark 3.76:1), which is the same split this repo already encodes. --destructive/--warning/--info are mapped to ink values and --hmi-alarm-hi/--hmi-alarm-lo to the fills, because the hues themselves cannot be labels: #C1272D at 12–14px is 3.66:1 on the field and #E8A317 is 1.36:1. What sits on an alarm plate is near-white (#F2F3F5 on #C1272D = 5.26:1) or near-black (#1A1D21 on #E8A317 = 7.80:1), and the ink pairs that replace them clear 4.5 on every plate they are actually painted on — #8F1D22 5.55:1 on the field / 6.45:1 on the card, dark #EF838A 5.39:1 / 4.66:1, #28506C 5.36:1 / 6.22:1, #6B4408 5.35:1 / 6.22:1, #1F5A41 5.06:1 / 5.88:1. Three sanctioned deviations from the control, all of them colour-role: --elev*/--key are none (no shadows); --success-soft is a flat grey plate, because a normal-state row must not be tinted at all; and .row.sel mark reads var(--mark-sel) with color: var(--mark-ink) instead of inheriting --primary-foreground — amber in light (5.88:1 against the selection bar, 7.80:1 for its own text), grey in dark (1.68:1 as a graphic, 5.37:1 as text). Near-white text on that amber plate would be 1.36:1, which is exactly the failure HP-HMI exists to prevent: the fill is allowed colour, the ink on it is not. The acknowledge device therefore implements the Rockwell sentence as a border blink plus a chip that blinks the fill, not as a filled text row — the row’s own secondary ink on #C1272D is 1.51:1 (light) and 2.67:1 (dark), so an alarm-filled row would be unreadable for half of every cycle. Its settled state, and the state prefers-reduced-motion leaves it in, is the acknowledged one: a solid alarm-colour border on the flat field, which is why the blink carries no information the static state does not.

  • Move 1Colour: tokens/colors.css collapses to a grey ramp plus exactly two alarm hues and one desaturated blue, and semantic/theme.css gains --hmi-* rows in both halves (:root and .dark) so no component or dark: utility is touched. The 61.3% light-and-glare-sensitivity claim on the risk line is the reason to do it first.
  • Move 2Depth and corner language: tokens/shadow.css sets --shadow-* to none and --ring-color to the 2px structure ink; tokens/radius.css drops the base radius to 4px, which moves every corner in the renderer — including the pristine components/ui/ primitives — with no component edit.
  • Move 3Rhythm and type: tokens/spacing.css keeps --target-row 40px and adds --target-bench 32px; tokens/typography.css registers --ff-hmi (Inter → system fallback) and the paired --text-13--line-height: 17px rung; tokens/motion.css gains --animate-alarm-ack (steps(1), one period ≤ 3 flashes/s). Markup costs: ResultItem.tsx needs the flow chevron span and ask-mode/BrowserProgressPanel.tsx the tank-bar block; ChatMessageBubble.tsx’s thinking steps swap the round glyph for a square outline. The acknowledgement machine is new state and not a restyle: useChatSession.ts would have to model ack/unack per failed tool, and that is the real line in this column.

Cost: LOW — it is the only direction in this lab that fits the existing light surface ramp and the current --success/--warning/--info pairs instead of fighting them. Every role name survives with a value change; nothing needs a wrapper element, a second registration layer, a main-process flag, or a restructure. The genuinely new work is one state field (acknowledged or not) and one keyframe.

Biggest risk: it looks deliberately plain, so it must be judged on whether an operator can triage a failed tool call in under a second — not on how it photographs. Every other mock here is auditioning on appearance; this one is auditioning on glance time, and a lab that scores on screenshots will rank it last. The honest counter-claim is that this is the only direction here that makes the app better for the 61.3% of low-vision users who report light and glare sensitivity: a mid-grey field with no tinted normal state and colour reserved for the abnormal is not a taste choice, it is the measured accommodation. Second risk, and it is real: an HP-HMI field at 13px on a desktop monitor is a control-room convention, and Qyther is a launcher that spends most of its life hidden.
