18c · EFIS Control Round — the container is the instrument, the control is the knob

Radius policy — the reduction is loudest in intent, not in value: every surface is `0px`, and interactive chrome alone takes `4px` (key caps, buttons, badges, the Ask pill, the tooltip, the mode tab). The progress bar and the switch are deliberately not pills — a pill is a web affordance and this system has none. Radius becomes a signal: if a corner is round, the thing inside it does something.

Provenance — the glass-cockpit electronic flight instrument display convention, taken from four published FAA sources by name: General Guidance Human Factors (FAA Office of Environment and Energy, DOT/FAA–EE–002/02.05-6), Ch. 3 §7 "Color", which maps features to hues — green for armed/engaged modes, cyan for selected data and selected values, magenta for the active route and flight plan — and warns explicitly that amber is easily confused; AC 25.1322-1, "Pilot-Display Considerations for Some Flight Management and Guidance Systems", which states that "Green is usually used to indicate 'normal' conditions" and that red, amber and yellow must be used consistently (red = warning, amber/yellow = caution); AC 25-11B, which names the escalation — "the progression from green to amber to red, representing increasing degrees of threat"; and Use of Color in ATC Displays (DOT/FAA–EE–022/25.05-18), whose reason for choosing cyan and magenta is that they are the two hues least likely to be confused with the rest of the set. Sentence wording above is this mock's paraphrase of those documents; the hue-to-state mapping is their published content. The eight hex values below are DERIVED for this mock from that mapping and are not from any of the four documents — no FAA advisory publishes a palette. Type is B612 / B612 Mono, SIL Open Font License 1.1, designed and legibility-tested for aircraft cockpit screens (validated with ENAC, the French civil-aviation university) and released by Airbus through the Polarsys Foundation.

Type plan — "B612", "Segoe UI Variable Text", system-ui, sans-serif for UI, "B612 Mono", ui-monospace, "Cascadia Mono", monospace for telemetry. B612 is OFL and therefore vendorable into a sandboxed renderer with no CDN, which is the only honest way to guarantee it here; on a machine without it the mock renders in Segoe UI and looks like a decent monospaced dashboard, not like this. Its differentiators are exactly what a 13 px telemetry stream needs and are shown in the banner specimen: a slashed zero (0Ø), separated 1/l/I, separated 5/S and 0/O, and open apertures so 18/88/38 do not collapse into a bead. Both are regular text typefaces, not condensed grotesques, and the display half is B612 regular at a large optical size.

Geometry — the oscilloscope/EFD graticule: 10 divisions horizontally × 8 vertically, each major division subdivided into fifths, and labels placed on every 5th tick only (shown under the banner — 80/60/40/20/0, not nine numbers). Rows are 28 px static readout / 40 px interactive, control floor 32 px; palette stays 800 × 72. Radius is 8/6/4/2 but instrument frames are square, and depth is never a shadow here: a raised surface is a reference-white 1 px frame with corner ticks, plus the graticule underneath it. Day and night are two token sets, one stylesheet, switched by the app's existing .dark class — the night block in this file is keyed on [data-theme="dark"], .dark to show that no second sheet is involved. Text recipe: --tracking .06em uppercase mono for every label, no italic, no weight below 400.

The rule that makes this honest — five status hues are not nameable at 13 px on their own, and two of them (amber and a desaturated green) merge for the ~8 % of men with red-green deficiency. So every coloured state carries its word beside the colour, always: WCAG 2.2 1.4.1 Use of Colour (colour must not be the only means of conveying information), the same rule the SCADA/HMI alarms literature states as "annunciate, then colour", and AC 25.1322-1's consistency requirement. Implemented below: the annunciator row prints ENGAGED / SELECTED / PLANNED / CAUTION / EXCEEDED next to each lamp, the tool trace prints ok / err / wait / plan next to each dot, and the gauge prints 2 of 3 as well as filling an arc. A state that is colour-only in this system is a defect, not a style.

Measured, not assumed — day mode is dark characters on a light ground per MIL-STD-1472H §5.18.2.2.1, with the §5.2.2.7 ratios (6:1 minimum, 10:1 preferred): ink #0b1116 on the #dfe5ea ground is 14.95:1 and on the day card #e7ecf0 15.96:1, so it clears even the preferred figure. The five law hues cannot be used in their night form on a light plate, so each was darkened until it clears 4.5:1 as 13 px text on the day card: engaged green #00683A 5.80:1, armed cyan #0C6377 5.76:1, planned magenta #7A2193 7.16:1, caution amber #7A5008 5.93:1, exceeded red #A6241C 6.11:1. Every pair was then re-checked against the plate it is actually painted on, including its own -soft tint over the darker --secondary surface, and that is where the day tints had to come down to 7 % alpha (worst case engaged on its own tint: 4.78:1 at 7 %, 4.22:1 at the night's 16 %) — a status tint that eats the 4.5 floor is precisely how a colour law decays into decoration. Night is the reverse: #E8EDF2 on the #04070A ground is 17.14:1, and on the night card the five read engaged 9.94, armed 9.51, caution 10.34, planned 5.48, exceeded 5.64. Two published-form values fail as text and are therefore kept as fills and rules only, said out loud rather than quietly used: exceeded red #E3352B measures 4.38:1 on the night card, so the text role is the lifted #F2564A (5.64:1 on the card, 4.89:1 on its own tint) while #E3352B stays the annunciator and guard-band colour; and the contextual muted #4A5560 measures 2.51:1, so it is the graticule, the disabled state and — lifted to #61717E, 3.79:1, clearing WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 control-boundary floor — --input, while muted text is #8FA0AE at 7.10:1. This direction earns its keep on exactly this discipline: the hue you are allowed to name is the hue you measured.

Lamps are state marks, not text — and WCAG 1.4.11 still asks 3:1 for anything that identifies a state, so the day annunciator fills are the day-dimmed versions of the night lamps rather than the night values on a light plate: engaged #0F8F53 3.48:1, armed #177F9E 3.87:1, planned #B846D4 3.63:1, caution #AD720C 3.40:1, exceeded #E23C31 3.59:1, each against the day card. Two structural floors are held deliberately: --border is a decorative divider and clears only 1.4 (day #A2AEB8 1.90:1 on the card, night #31404D 1.79:1 — the same split this repo already encodes), while --input is a control boundary and clears 3:1 (day 4.62, night 3.79). The tightest pairing the law produces is recorded as a constraint, not hidden: magenta is the darkest of the five, so a plan pill belongs on a raised plate (4.64:1 on the card, 4.06:1 on the popover plate it does not use) and never on the darker steps surface at 3.86:1 — which is exactly the kind of plate-and-hue disagreement a named colour law makes visible instead of merely ugly.

  • Move 1 — name the lawRewrite the status pairs in src/renderer/styles/semantic/theme.css as the five-state law: --engaged, --armed, --planned, --caution, --exceeded, each with a -foreground and a -soft half in both the :root and .dark blocks, plus the --*-lamp fill that is deliberately not a text colour. Register them in the single @theme inline (theme.css:19) as --color-engaged … so components get text-engaged / bg-caution-soft. Hue primitives stay fills: node tmp/token-refs.mjs already fails any --color-*-500 used as text, which is this same invariant with a different name — adopt the law and that gate becomes the enforcement, not the suggestion.
  • Move 2 — give every status its word, in markupBind the mapping at its source instead of at the surface: one STATUS_LAW table (src/main/services/pi/toolbox.ts emits details.status, which today is a free string per tool) and a renderer utils/statusLaw.ts that returns {hue, label} as a pair, so a component cannot render a colour without the label. Touch src/renderer/components/ask-mode/BrowserProgressPanel.tsx (annunciator row, gauge), ask-mode/ThinkingSteps.tsx (step states), ask-mode/ChatErrorBanner.tsx and contexts/ToastContext.tsx (the caution band + its word). Type-only additions to src/shared/ipc/ so main, preload and renderer share the pair — the four-site IPC rule still applies, five where src/renderer/types.d.ts already names the channel.
  • Move 3 — vendor the face and set the graticuleAdd B612 + B612 Mono to public/ and declare them in tokens/typography.css (--font-sans/--font-mono plus a --tracking-annunciator); graticule geometry and the 28 px static row go in tokens/spacing.css beside --target-row; the lamp edge/bloom and the reference frame in tokens/shadow.css; the single 150 ms annunciator change in tokens/motion.css as an --animate-* shorthand with its none line in globals.css:25-38. radius.css goes to 4/2 px on instrument frames and keeps 8 px on the card.

Cost: MEDIUM — mostly a semantic-pair explosion in semantic/theme.css: five states × (-foreground + -soft + -lamp) × two theme halves, all of which must pass the contrast gate independently, plus one vendored font family pair and a label column added to every component that currently announces a state by tint. No wrapper elements, no new stylesheet, no main-process change.

Biggest risk: the mapping is a contract you must keep, not a palette. The moment magenta means "pretty" instead of "active route / plan", the whole system becomes decoration and an operator misreads a tool failure as a plan step — which is the failure the FAA documents exist to prevent, and the one this direction makes unforgivable because it has a named meaning to check against. Concretely, Qyther's agent reports status as free-text details.status strings from 32 tools in src/main/services/pi/toolbox.ts; adopting this law means each of those strings gets one named state and a label, that the list is frozen by a test the way toolbox.test.ts froze the names, and that a new tool with no row is a build failure rather than a hue someone picked. The second-order risk is the one the ATC report already flags: amber reads as caution to this law and as "selected" to half the team, so the law has to be documented where components can be checked against it, not remembered.
